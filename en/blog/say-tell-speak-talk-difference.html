say, tell, speak or talk — four verbs that are easy to mix up 🗣️

26 October 2026 · Nodar

In many languages, all four of these are just one or two everyday verbs for “saying” or “talking”. In English, each one has its own place — and a mistake is heard straight away.

🗣️ say — what you said (the words)
👤 tell — who you told (a person)
🌐 speak — languages; formal speech
💬 talk — conversation, dialogue
In this article
  1. say vs. tell — the main rule
  2. Fixed phrases with tell
  3. speak vs. talk
  4. What we say on the phone
  5. Test yourself

say vs. tell — the main rule

This one rule covers most cases:

✅ tell + a person → tell me, tell him, tell your mother
✅ say + words → say hello, say something, say that…
❌ He said me the truth.
✅ He told me the truth.

❌ She told that she was tired.
✅ She said that she was tired.
✅ She told me that she was tired.

If you still want say with a person, you need to:

✅ He said to me, "I'm leaving." — slightly more bookish

Fixed phrases with tell

These expressions always take tell — learn them as whole chunks:

✅ tell the truth · tell a lie · tell a story · tell a joke
✅ tell the time · tell the difference · tell someone your name
❌ Can you say me the time?
✅ Can you tell me the time?

speak vs. talk

speak — languages, formal situations, one-way speech (for example, from a stage).

✅ Do you speak English? — with languages it's almost always speak.
✅ May I speak to Mr Brown? — on the phone, formally.
✅ She spoke at the conference.

talk — two-way, relaxed conversation. It's more common in everyday speech.

✅ We talked for two hours.
✅ Let's talk about it tomorrow.
✅ Stop talking in class!

Both take the same prepositions: talk/speak to or with someone, about something.

⚠️ I want to speak with you about the project. — correct, but it sounds formal.
✅ I want to talk to you about the project. — natural, everyday.

What we say on the phone

This is the situation where a mistake stands out the most:

✅ — Hello, can I speak to Nino, please?
✅ — Speaking. / This is Nino. — that's me. (Not “I am Nino”!)
✅ — Who's calling? — the polite way to ask who it is.

Test yourself

1. Please ___ me what happened.
2. He didn't ___ anything.
3. How many languages do you ___?
4. We need to ___ about your homework.

Answers: 1. tell (me → a person). 2. say (anything → words). 3. speak (languages). 4. talk (two-way conversation; speak is also correct, but sounds more formal).

These four verbs come up dozens, even hundreds of times in everyday conversation — which is exactly why they show most clearly whether you're using English freely or translating in your head.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between say and tell?

Tell is followed by a person (tell me, tell him, tell your mother), say by words (say hello, say something, say that…). Correct: He told me the truth and She said that she was tired. If you use say with a person, you need to: He said to me.

What's the difference between speak and talk?

Speak is more formal and is almost always the verb used with languages (Do you speak English?), and also for public speaking — She spoke at the conference. Talk is a two-way, relaxed conversation: We talked for two hours.

What do you answer on the phone to “Can I speak to Nino?”

If you are Nino, the answer is “Speaking.” or “This is Nino.” “I am Nino” isn't said in English in this situation, and it immediately shows that English isn't your first language.
