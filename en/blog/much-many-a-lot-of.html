much, many or a lot of — and why “advices” is wrong 🔢

26 October 2026 · Nodar

In many languages, one word covers both “much” and “many”. In English you need to know two things: whether the thing is countable or not, and whether you're dealing with a question, a negative or a positive statement.

In this article
  1. Step one: is it countable?
  2. much / many / a lot of
  3. a few / a little — and their “evil twins”
  4. how much or how many
  5. Test yourself

Step one: is it countable?

🔢 Countable — can go into the plural: books, friends, hours, ideas
🌊 Uncountable — has no plural: water, money, time, information, advice, work

Watch out: some words are uncountable in English even though their equivalents sound perfectly natural in the plural in many other languages.

❌ I need some advices. / He gave me two informations.
✅ I need some advice. · some information
✅ a piece of advice · a piece of information — if you want to say “one”

Here's a list worth learning by heart: news, furniture, luggage, equipment, homework, research, progress, money, information, advice — all of them are uncountable.

⚠️ One nuance: hair is uncountable when you mean hair as a whole (She has long hair), but a single strand is countable: two hairs. The same logic applies to coffee: two coffees = two cups.

❌ The news are good.
✅ The news is good. — news is uncountable, even though it ends in “s”!

much / many / a lot of

✅ many + countable → many books, many people
✅ much + uncountable → much time, much money
✅ a lot of + both → a lot of books, a lot of money

The key practical rule, which textbooks often mention only in passing: much sounds more natural in questions and negatives — in positive statements, a lot of is the better choice. many, on the other hand, is perfectly normal in positive statements too: Many people came.

✅ Do you have much time? — question: natural
✅ I don't have much time. — negative: natural
⚠️ I have much time. — grammatically correct, but it sounds odd
✅ I have a lot of time. — this is what people actually say

a few / a little — and their “evil twins”

Here one tiny a changes the whole mood:

✅ I have a few friends here. — some friends (positive: that's enough)
✅ I have few friends here. — hardly anyone (negative: not enough)

✅ There's a little milk left. — some milk is left (it's enough)
✅ There's little milk left. — there's almost none left.

a few → with countable nouns. a little → with uncountable nouns.

how much or how many

✅ How many students are there?
✅ How much time do we have?
✅ How much is it? — asking the price (money is uncountable)

Test yourself

Word bank: much · many · a lot of · a piece of

1. I don't have ___ money left — only 5 dollars.
2. She has ___ friends in Berlin — more than twenty.
3. ___ people came to the party — the room was full.
4. Can I give you ___ advice?

Answers: 1. much (a lot of is also correct) — negative + uncountable. 2. a lot of or many. 3. A lot of / Many. 4. a piece of — not an advice (there's a separate article on articles).

Countability is a topic you can't cover in a single lesson — your list of uncountable nouns grows little by little. On the other hand, once you understand the difference in the pairs a few/few and a little/little, you won't mix them up again: one tiny a, and the mood flips.

Frequently asked questions

Why is “advices” wrong?

In English, advice is an uncountable noun, so it has no plural. Correct: some advice or a piece of advice. The same goes for information, news, furniture, luggage and homework.

When do we use much and when a lot of?

much sounds more natural in questions and negatives (Do you have much time? I don't have much time). In positive statements, a lot of is the better choice: I have a lot of time. many, on the other hand, is perfectly normal in positive statements too: Many people came.

What's the difference between a few and few?

a few is positive: some, enough. few is negative: not many, almost none. The same logic applies to a little and little.
