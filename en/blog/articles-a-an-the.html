a, an or the — English articles made simple 📰

26 October 2026 · Nodar

Many languages have no articles at all, and others use them in different places. That is exactly why a, an and the are one of the most common sources of mistakes — sadly, even at C1 level.

The good news: in most cases, three questions are enough.

In this article
  1. Three questions that settle most cases
  2. a / an — “some, one of many”
  3. the — “that very one”
  4. When you don't need an article at all
  5. Countries and cities
  6. Check yourself

Three questions that settle most cases

1️⃣ Does the listener know exactly which one I mean? If yes — the. (Both countable and uncountable: the book, the salt.)
2️⃣ No — and is it a countable noun in the singular? Then — a / an.
3️⃣ No — and is it uncountable or plural? No article needed. (I like music. I like books.)

a / an — “some, one of many”

When you mention something for the first time, or when it doesn't matter which one it is.

✅ I bought a book. — some book; the listener doesn't know which one.
✅ She is a teacher. — with a job in the singular you almost always need a/an (the exception is a unique position: She was elected president). In the plural — nothing: They are teachers.

a or an? The sound decides, not the letter:

✅ an hour — the “h” is silent, so it starts with a vowel sound · a university — you hear “ju”, a consonant sound
✅ an MBA — you hear “em” · a European country — you hear “ju”

the — “that very one”

When we both know which one we're talking about.

✅ I bought a book. The book was expensive. — the second time, it's already “that” book.
✅ Close the door, please. — there's one door in the room, and we both know which.
✅ The sun, the moon, the internet — each one is the only one in the world.

You also need the with superlatives (the best, the tallest), ordinal numbers (the first, the second), musical instruments (play the piano) and before many geographical names (the Black Sea, the Caucasus, the USA).

When you don't need an article at all

This is where an extra the often slips in.

⚠️ I like the music. — correct if you mean specific music (at a party, for example); for the general meaning, see below.
✅ I like music. — music in general.

❌ The life is beautiful.
✅ Life is beautiful. — life in general.

⚠️ I go to the school every day. — correct, but it means something else: I go to the building (as a parent, for example).
✅ I go to school every day. — I'm a student.

The last pair is especially interesting. go to school / hospital / church / bed / work — without an article, it means the purpose (I study, I'm treated, I pray, I sleep, I work). With an article — just the place itself (the building, a specific bed).

✅ My son is in hospital. — he's ill and being treated.
✅ I'm at the hospital visiting a friend. — I'm in the building, as a visitor.

This is the British rule. In American English, the is used in both cases: in the hospital. The rest (at work, in bed) is the same in both; for at school, American English often says in school (meaning “is a student”).

Countries and cities

✅ Georgia, France, Germany, Italy — no article
✅ Tbilisi, Paris, Berlin — cities have no article either
✅ the USA, the UK, the Netherlands, the Philippines — a plural or a “union”

Check yourself

Fill in a, an, the or nothing (—):

1. She wants to be ___ doctor.
2. Can you pass me ___ salt? (you're sitting at the same table)
3. I don't drink ___ coffee.
4. We stayed in ___ hotel near ___ sea.

Answers: 1. a — a job. 2. the — we both know which one. 3. — (nothing) — coffee in general. 4. a hotel (first mention), the sea (it's the only sea there).

Articles are a topic you don't learn from a book — you learn them by ear. The more you read and listen in English, the more often you'll “just feel” what goes where.

Frequently asked questions

When do we use a and when an?

It depends on the sound, not the letter. If the word starts with a vowel sound — an (an hour, an MBA). If it starts with a consonant sound — a (a university, a European country).

Why is it “I like music” and not “I like the music”?

When we talk about something in general, we don't need an article. the is used when we both know exactly which one we mean.

What's the difference between “go to school” and “go to the school”?

Without the article it means the purpose — I'm a student. With the article — I'm just going to the building, for example as a parent.
