Job interview in English — questions, the STAR method and phrases to buy time 💼

26 October 2026 · Nodar

A job interview in English scares almost everyone at first. In reality, it is the most predictable conversation you will ever have — the questions are usually the same.

“Tell me about yourself” — the most important 90 seconds

This is not your life story — it's a short presentation in three parts: now → past → future.

✅ Currently, I work as a project coordinator at a logistics company.
✅ Before that, I spent three years in customer support, where I learned how to…
✅ Now I'm looking for a role where I can use both my organisational and language skills.

60–90 seconds is enough. A common mistake is starting from your childhood — the interviewer only cares about your professional path.

Five questions you will almost certainly be asked

❓ Why do you want to work here?
❓ What are your strengths / weaknesses?
❓ Tell me about a difficult situation you handled.
❓ Where do you see yourself in five years?
❓ Do you have any questions for us? — ⚠️ “No” comes across as a lack of interest.

STAR — a structure that works for every “give me an example” question

When they ask for an example, answer in four steps. In many companies, especially international ones, this is considered the standard:

Situation — what was happening · “Last year our main supplier cancelled…”
Task — what your job was · “I had to find a replacement in two weeks.”
Action — what you did · “I contacted five companies and negotiated…”
Result — what came out of it · “We didn't miss a single delivery.”

Always name the result — ideally with a number. This is the part candidates skip most often.

Your weakness — how not to hurt yourself

“I'm a perfectionist” is so worn out that it has the opposite effect. The formula that works: a real weakness + what you are doing to fix it.

✅ I used to take on too much myself instead of delegating. Now I… — and then a concrete step.

Phrases to buy time

If you didn't understand the question or need a few seconds to think of an answer, that's normal. The main thing is not to go silent.

✅ That's a good question. Let me think for a second.
✅ Sorry, could you rephrase that?
✅ Just to make sure I understood — you're asking about…?

Your questions — this is where you stand out

Answering the last question with “No, thank you” signals a lack of interest. Prepare two or three:

✅ What does a typical week look like in this role?
✅ How do you measure success in the first six months?
✅ What are the next steps in the process? — always at the end.
✅ If they've already explained everything: You've actually answered everything — could you just tell me about the next steps?

How to prepare

Preparing for an interview doesn't mean memorising a script — a memorised answer is obvious straight away. It's better to have three key words for each question and build the sentences yourself as you speak.

In practice it works like this: we spend one or two lessons on a realistic mock interview — I ask questions based on the job you're applying for, and then we go through the recording together. A rehearsal like this usually changes a lot.

An interview often starts with a few minutes of light conversation — about the weather, your journey, your day. For that part, small talk phrases will come in handy.

Test yourself

Which answer is better, and why?

1. — What's your biggest weakness?
   (a) I'm a perfectionist. · (b) I used to take on too much myself. Now I plan who does what at the start.
2. — Do you have any questions for us?
   (a) No, thank you. · (b) Yes — how do you measure success in the first six months?
3. — Tell me about yourself.
   (a) I was born in Kutaisi and went to school… · (b) Currently I work as a coordinator. Before that…

Answers: in all three cases — (b). (1) A real weakness + a step to fix it. (2) The question shows interest. (3) Your professional path, not your life story.

An interview is one of the rare situations where the result of preparation shows directly and clearly: the same questions, the same structure — only your content changes. The point of rehearsing is to replace fear with focus: you don't come up with your answers on the spot, you have already tried them out.

Frequently asked questions

How do I answer “Tell me about yourself”?

In three parts: now → past → future. 60–90 seconds is enough. Starting from your childhood is a common mistake — the interviewer only cares about your professional path.

What is the STAR method?

Situation, Task, Action, Result — a four-step structure for when you are asked for a specific example. Always name the result, ideally with a number.

What do I say when they ask at the end if I have any questions?

Never just say “No” — it comes across as a lack of interest. Prepare two or three questions, for example: “How do you measure success in the first six months?”
