Online English level test — 20 questions, A1 to B2 📝

26 October 2026 · Nodar

“What level am I?” — this is the question I hear most often. Some people remember having an “intermediate” level at school, others watch films with subtitles but struggle to speak. Nobody really knows where to start.

Below is a short test: 20 questions, about five minutes. The result gives you a rough idea of your level — from A1 to B2 — and points to the topics worth focusing on.

How the test works

The questions are ordered by difficulty: the first five are A1 level, the next five A2, then B1 and finally B2. Each question has three answer options, and only one of them is correct.

A level counts as passed if you answer at least four of its five questions correctly and all the levels below it are passed too. So lucky guesses can hardly push your result up.

Three tips to get an accurate result:

1. Don't use a dictionary or a translator.
2. If you're not sure, choose the option that seems more correct to you. You need to answer at least 15 questions to see your result.
3. Don't think for too long. Your first, instinctive answer often reflects what you know most accurately.

The test: 20 questions

1. My sister ___ a doctor.

With the third person singular (he, she, it, my sister) — is.

2. ___ you like coffee?

A Present Simple question with you starts with the auxiliary do; does is only for he/she/it.

3. There ___ two books on the table.

With a plural (two books) — there are.

4. I get up ___ seven o'clock.

With clock times we use the preposition at: at seven o'clock, at noon.

5. She ___ in London.

In the Present Simple, the verb takes -s with he/she/it: she lives.

6. Yesterday we ___ to the cinema.

yesterday points to a finished past — Past Simple: went. gone is the third form and isn't used without an auxiliary verb.

7. This bag is ___ than that one.

With two-syllable adjectives ending in -y, the comparative changes -y to -ier: heavy → heavier.

8. Look outside! It ___, so take an umbrella.

Look outside! and “take an umbrella” tell us it's raining right now — Present Continuous: is raining.

9. How ___ sugar do you want?

sugar is an uncountable noun, so — how much. how many is only for countable nouns.

10. I'm tired because I ___ well last night.

last night is a finished period in the past — Past Simple: didn't sleep. The Present Perfect isn't used with a time reference like this.

11. I ___ in this city since 2019.

since 2019 — the action started in the past and is still going on: have lived. Many languages use the present tense here, but English needs the Present Perfect.

12. If I ___ more time, I would learn Spanish.

An imagined condition (Second Conditional): if + past tense, and would in the main clause: If I had… I would…

13. The letter ___ yesterday.

A letter doesn't send anything itself — somebody sends it. Passive voice, past tense: was sent.

14. I'm looking forward to ___ you.

In look forward to, to is a preposition, not part of an infinitive, so it's followed by the -ing form: looking forward to seeing.

15. When I arrived at the cinema, the film ___.

The film started before I arrived — an action that happened before another action in the past: Past Perfect, had already started.

16. I wish I ___ harder when I was at school.

Regret about the past (“if only I'd studied”) — wish + Past Perfect: I wish I had studied.

17. By the time you get here, we ___ dinner.

An action that will be finished before a specific moment in the future — Future Perfect: will have finished.

18. He denied ___ the window.

deny is followed by the -ing form: deny doing something. The same goes for enjoy, avoid, admit, suggest.

19. You ___ have told me earlier — I could have helped you!

A reproach for something that wasn't done (“you ought to have told me”) — should have + third form. must have expresses a guess (“you probably told me”).

20. It's high time we ___ home — it's almost midnight.

After It's (high) time comes a past tense, but the meaning is that something should happen right now: It's high time we went — “we really should go now”.

What your result means

The levels are based on the European scale, the CEFR. A1 and A2 are the basic levels, B1 and B2 are independent user levels, and C1 and C2 are proficient levels. I've written about each level in detail, with examples, in the article on English levels.

Getting through one level takes roughly 150–200 hours, and more at the higher levels. The real timeframe depends on how much and how regularly you study; there's a separate article on that — how long it takes to learn English.

What this test can't measure

The test only checks grammar. But knowing a language is made up of four skills: reading, writing, listening and speaking. It often happens that someone scores B1 on a test but speaks at A2 level — or the other way round: they speak fluently but make grammar mistakes.

So this result is a guide, not a certificate. An official level is confirmed by an exam such as IELTS, TOEFL or Cambridge.

Frequently asked questions

How can I find out what level my English is?

Answer the 20 questions on this page: the test takes about five minutes and gives you a rough idea of your grammar level, from A1 to B2. An official level is confirmed by an exam such as IELTS, TOEFL or Cambridge.

How accurate is an online test?

The test only checks grammar, so the result is a guide. It can't measure speaking, listening or writing, and your level in those skills may be different.

How is the result calculated?

The test has four blocks, A1, A2, B1 and B2, with five questions each. A level counts as passed if you answer at least four of its five questions correctly and all the levels below it are passed too.

Why are there no C1 and C2 questions?

At C1 and C2, the main differences lie in speaking, writing and depth of vocabulary, and 20 grammar questions can't show that. To determine these levels, you need a live conversation or an official exam.
