English for studying abroad — what level do universities require? 🎓

26 October 2026 · Nodar

“I want to do my bachelor's in Germany, on an English-taught programme — what level do I need?” “They want 6.5 for a master's in London, and I'm B1 — can I make it by September?” I hear these two questions every spring, and in summer — already in a panicky tone.

English for studying abroad means two different things: the score your application asks for, and the level you need to actually follow a lecture. Below I cover both — with numbers, but with one caveat: requirements change, and the final word is always on the page of the specific programme.

English for studying abroad — what level universities require

Most universities ask for B2 on English-taught programmes; more demanding ones (medicine, law, journalism, some master's programmes) ask for C1. But you can't just write “B2” on your application: the university needs an exam score, and here's how one maps onto the other.

📊 B2 ≈ IELTS 5.5–6.5 · TOEFL iBT ≈ 72–94
C1 ≈ IELTS 7.0–8.0 · TOEFL iBT ≈ 95–120
— the mapping is approximate: someone right on the boundary can land on one level or the other.

One detail that often goes unnoticed: many programmes list not only an overall score but also a minimum for each section — for example, “6.5, with no section below 6.0”. If you have 5.5 in Writing, an overall 6.5 won't save you.

Which exams are accepted and what scores are usually required

Four exams appear on almost every list: IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, Duolingo English Test and Cambridge C1 Advanced. Which one to choose is decided first by which ones your programme accepts, and then by which format suits you.

  • United Kingdom. For a bachelor's, IELTS is usually 6.0–6.5; for a master's, 6.5–7.0. TOEFL and Cambridge are accepted too. Visa requirements are a separate topic, and the university will give you that information itself.
  • USA. TOEFL iBT is mostly 80–100, or IELTS 6.5–7.0. Many universities accept the Duolingo English Test — the typical requirement is 105–120. Where competition is fierce, the bar is higher too.
  • Germany, English-taught programmes. Often IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL iBT 80–90. Some universities also accept C1 Advanced, some accept Duolingo, some don't.

A quick word on format: you take the Duolingo English Test from home and it's cheaper, while for IELTS and TOEFL you usually go to a test centre. Cambridge C1 Advanced has one advantage: the certificate doesn't expire (though some universities still ask for a result from the last 2–3 years — check). IELTS and TOEFL results are valid for two years — more on that below.

And once again: these are guidelines, not rules. The page of the specific programme always beats any article, this one included.

Germany: English or German — the most common misunderstanding

This question deserves its own section, because every year I meet someone who is preparing for IELTS but plans to apply to a German-taught programme.

The rule is simple: they ask for a certificate in the language the programme is taught in. English-taught bachelor's and master's programmes need an English certificate; German-taught ones need German: TestDaF (usually 4 in every section) or DSH-2. An IELTS 7.5 won't help you on a German-taught programme.

There's an in-between option too: an English-taught programme that also asks for basic German (often A2 or B1) for everyday life. So read both lines on the programme page — “Sprachnachweis Englisch” (proof of English) and “Deutschkenntnisse” (knowledge of German).

⚠️ "I'm doing IELTS because I want to study medicine in Germany." — a planning mistake, not a language one: in Germany, medicine is as a rule taught in German.
✅ "I need TestDaF for medicine — and English for the textbooks and conferences."

Score and lecture — two different things

Here's something agencies rarely tell you. IELTS 6.5 means your application will go through. It doesn't mean that on Monday at nine in the morning you'll fully understand a lecturer who speaks fast and with a Scottish accent.

Three things you need at university every day, which the exam only partly measures:

  • Listening to a fast-paced lecture for 90 minutes — and taking notes at the same time. IELTS Listening is 30 minutes and you hear the recording only once; a lecture is heard only once too, but it's three times longer.
  • Writing essays of 2,000–3,000 words, with sources cited. In the exam you write 250 words in 40 minutes — that's a different skill.
  • Speaking in seminars: arguing a point, asking questions, summarising someone else's view. Here nobody asks you to "Describe a memorable trip".

So if you have time, aim for a level, not a score: at a solid B2, 6.0–6.5 is realistic; jumping from B1 to 6.5 with templates is not.

A realistic timeline — and one typical mistake

I don't make promises about how long it will take, but I'll share the logic of the calculation. As an international guideline, one level takes roughly 150–200 hours, and more at the higher levels. Find out three numbers: where you are (measured by a test, not by feel), where you need to be, and how many hours a week you'll realistically give it — lessons, homework and independent work together.

📊 Hours per level ÷ your hours per week = number of weeks
For example: 180 ÷ 6 = 30 weeks for one level
— a guideline, not a promise: the pace depends on your starting level, how regular you are, and on you as a person.

And two practical rules. First: book the exam date only after a practice test — after a full, timed test that gives you a result close to your target. A date booked “for motivation” often turns into paying the fee twice.

Second — the most common mistake: someone at B1 who prepares “for the exam” from day one. Strategies, essay templates, Task 1 frameworks — all of that only polishes what you already have. No template turns B1 into C1. Level first, then technique; both together — only in the last few months.

Documents and deadlines — when to take the exam

IELTS and TOEFL results are valid for two years, and Duolingo's for two as well. This leads to two opposite mistakes: some people take the exam too early, and by the time they enrol the certificate has expired; others take it too late, and the result doesn't arrive in time for the application deadline.

Count backwards: take the programme's application deadline (in the UK and US it's often in winter; in Germany, for the winter semester, usually in July), subtract the time it takes to get your result (from a few days to two weeks) and a spare attempt in case your score isn't enough. What's left is your real exam date.

And check whether the university counts the two-year validity from the enrolment date or the application date — these are two different dates.

Test yourself

✍️ Test yourself: answer five questions before you start preparing.
1. What language is your programme taught in — and are you preparing for a certificate in that language?
2. Do you know the exact requirement from the programme page — both the overall score and the minimum for each section?
3. Was your current level measured by a test, or by “I'm probably B2”?
4. Is your target score close to what you got on your last practice test?
5. Have you put the application deadline, the wait for results and a spare attempt in your calendar?
If you answered “no” to at least three — sort those points out first, then book the exam.

One of my students got into one of Europe's leading universities (as they told me themselves), and their path started the same way: first an accurate diagnosis of their level, then a plan, and only at the end — the exam date. Frequently asked questions

What level of English do universities abroad require?

Most universities ask for B2 on English-taught programmes, more demanding programmes for C1. B2 corresponds roughly to IELTS 5.5–6.5, C1 to 7.0–8.0. Check the exact requirement on the page of your specific programme — it changes.

Do I need IELTS or a German certificate to study in Germany?

You need a certificate in the language the programme is taught in. English-taught programmes need an English certificate (often IELTS 6.0–6.5 or TOEFL iBT 80–90), German-taught ones TestDaF or DSH. IELTS won't help you on a German-taught programme.

Which exams do universities accept?

Most often IELTS Academic, TOEFL iBT, the Duolingo English Test and Cambridge C1 Advanced. In the UK, IELTS is usually 6.0–6.5 for a bachelor's and 6.5–7.0 for a master's; in the US, mostly TOEFL iBT 80–100 or IELTS 6.5–7.0. Your programme's page says which ones it accepts.

How long are IELTS and TOEFL results valid?

Two years; the Duolingo English Test also two. The Cambridge C1 Advanced certificate doesn't expire, though some universities still ask for a result from the last 2–3 years. Count backwards: take the application deadline, subtract the wait for results and a spare attempt — and check whether the university counts the two years from the enrolment date or the application date.
