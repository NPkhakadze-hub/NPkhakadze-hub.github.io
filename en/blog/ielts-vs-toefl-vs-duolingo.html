IELTS, TOEFL or Duolingo — which test to choose, and for which country 🎓

26 October 2026 · Nodar

“Should I take IELTS or TOEFL? And what's this Duolingo test everyone keeps mentioning?” I hear this question from almost everyone who is getting ready to study or work abroad. The answer comes in two parts: first it depends on who has to accept your result, and only then on which test “suits” you better.

Below I compare all three exams and look at Germany, the USA, Canada and the UK one by one. One note before we start: fees, deadlines and requirements change, so every number you read here is approximate — check the official website before you make a final decision.

Question first, test second: who needs your score?

The most common mistake is choosing the test first (“My friend took IELTS, so I'll take it too”) and only then looking for who will accept it. The right order is the other way round. Open the page of the university, programme or visa office where you have to submit your result and find the exact list: which tests they accept and what minimum score they require.

If you have three programmes in mind, write all three sets of requirements into one table — very often it turns out that one test works for all of them.

⚠️ "I will pass the IELTS exam next month." — Correct, but it means “I will succeed”, so you are promising a result.
✅ "I'm taking the IELTS exam next month." — take an exam = sit the exam (the process); pass = do it successfully.

IELTS — the classic choice, in two versions

IELTS has two versions: Academic — for university — and General Training — for migration and work visas. The test takes about 2 hours and 45 minutes: Listening (about 30 minutes), Reading (60), Writing (60) and Speaking (11–14 minutes) — the speaking part is with a live examiner, not a computer.

Scores are “bands” from 0 to 9, in half steps (6.0, 6.5, 7.0…). According to the official website, results for the computer-delivered version arrive in 1–5 days, for the paper version in up to 13 days. IELTS recommends that a result be treated as valid for two years; some organisations accept older ones, so check this with whoever receives your result too. The fee varies by country — look up the current amount on your local test centre's page.

❌ "My IELTS score is 6,5 band."
✅ "I got a 6.5 in IELTS." / "My overall band score is 6.5." — In English, decimals are written with a point, not a comma.

TOEFL iBT — academic English, on a new scale

TOEFL iBT is an American academic test and checks all four skills — entirely on a computer; even in the speaking section you talk into a microphone. The official website allows about two hours for the test. You can take it at a test centre or from home (Home Edition).

An important change: since January 2026, TOEFL scores are on a scale from 1 to 6, in half steps, and map directly onto CEFR levels (for example, 4–4.5 = B2, 5–5.5 = C1). The old 0–120 score will still be printed on the score report as a “comparable” score until January 2028, so that universities can convert their old requirements. So if a programme asks for “TOEFL 90”, that's the old scale — look up the new equivalent on the ETS website.

Your result appears in your ETS account 3 days after the test and is valid for two years. According to ETS, TOEFL is accepted by more than 13,000 institutions in over 160 countries.

❌ "I have TOEFL certificate 100 points."
✅ "I have a TOEFL score of 100." — The result is called a score (a score of 100); a certificate is a separate document. And don't forget the article.

Duolingo English Test — fast, from home, but not accepted everywhere

The Duolingo English Test (DET) is a fully online test: you take it from home, with a webcam and your ID. According to the official website, the whole process takes about an hour (5 minutes of setup and ID check, a 45-minute adaptive test, and 10 minutes of writing and speaking samples), and results arrive within 2 days. Scores range from 10 to 160. DET publishes its own comparison: for example, 120 corresponds roughly to IELTS 6.5 — the exact table, with CEFR levels, is on their website.

According to the website, the test is accepted by more than 6,500 institutions. That's a big number, but there is a trap here: “accepted by a university” and “accepted by an immigration office” are two different things. DET works mainly for university admissions; in visa and immigration procedures that require a language test it usually doesn't appear.

❌ "I made the Duolingo test at home and I had 120 points."
✅ "I took the Duolingo English Test at home and got 120." — You take a test (in British English you'll also hear do, but never make), and you get a score.

Country by country: who accepts what

Germany. English-taught programmes usually ask for IELTS Academic or TOEFL; some universities accept DET and some don't, so be sure to check the programme page. For the visa itself an English test is not normally required — the university's admission letter is what matters. And if the programme is taught in German, you'll need a German certificate instead of an English one.

USA. All three tests are most widely accepted here: TOEFL is historically the “American” test, most institutions accept IELTS, and DET became popular precisely at American universities. A student visa doesn't require a separate language test — the university's requirement is what counts.

Canada. Most universities accept IELTS Academic and TOEFL, and many accept DET too. For immigration programmes (permanent residence, for example), however, there is a separate list of approved tests, which includes the General Training version of IELTS, but not TOEFL or DET. In other words, “studying” and “staying” are two different questions with two different answers.

United Kingdom. The strictest case. For visa purposes the government only accepts approved SELT tests, including a special version — IELTS for UKVI — which is no different in content from regular IELTS but is taken at a separately registered centre. TOEFL and DET are not on this list. A university may accept other tests for its own admissions, but you need to check that with the specific programme and the current list on gov.uk.

Which one “suits” you — three practical questions

When the receiving side accepts two or three tests equally, your own strengths and weaknesses decide.

A live examiner or a microphone? In IELTS you speak with a person — that helps if talking comes easily to you. In TOEFL and DET you speak into a microphone, against the clock; some people find that calmer, others the opposite.

How much time do you have? You can take DET today and have your result in two days. IELTS and TOEFL need registration and a booked date, and usually a test centre (TOEFL Home Edition aside) — if your deadline is tight, that's an argument too.

What kind of English do you find hard? IELTS Listening includes British and Australian accents, and Reading uses British spelling; TOEFL is built mainly around American academic English. If you're used to American English from TV series, keep this in mind.

❌ "I'm preparing to IELTS since two months."
✅ "I've been preparing for IELTS for two months." — prepare for; a length of time takes for, a starting point takes since.

What stays the same, whichever test you choose

All three tests check the same thing: whether you can understand and express ideas in English. Test “tricks” — managing your time, question types, essay structure — can be learned relatively quickly with targeted preparation, but no trick can replace vocabulary, grammar and fluency in speaking. So before you pick a test, find out your level first: if you're at B2, preparing for any of the three is realistic; at A2 the work is still on the language, not on the test.

Check yourself

Choose the correct option. Each item has one answer.

1. For UK visa purposes, the government accepts: (a) the regular Duolingo English Test (b) IELTS for UKVI (c) TOEFL Home Edition
2. Which version of IELTS is on the approved list for Canadian immigration programmes? (a) Academic (b) General Training
3. Since 2026, what scale is the main TOEFL iBT score on? (a) 0–120 (b) 0–9 (c) 1–6
4. "I ______ the IELTS exam last Saturday, and I'm still waiting for the result." (a) passed (b) took (c) made
5. Which of the three tests is taken entirely from home, with no live examiner, in about one hour? (a) IELTS Academic (b) TOEFL iBT (c) Duolingo English Test

Answers: 1. b — only approved SELT tests, including IELTS for UKVI. 2. b — General Training; Academic is for university. 3. c — 1–6, in half steps; 0–120 remains only as a comparable score until January 2028. 4. b — took: the speaker doesn't know the result yet, so passed doesn't work; make an exam doesn't exist in English (in British English you'll also hear do an exam). 5. c — TOEFL has an at-home version, but it takes about two hours; in IELTS the speaking part is with a live examiner.

One practical tip: before you register for any test, do the free sample test for each option on your shortlist — IELTS, TOEFL and DET all have one — and see where you feel more comfortable. Frequently asked questions

IELTS, TOEFL or Duolingo — which one is easier?

None of them is “easy” — all three test all four skills. The difference is the format: in IELTS you speak with a live examiner, in TOEFL and DET you speak into a microphone; DET takes up to an hour, IELTS and TOEFL much longer. Choose the one the receiving institution accepts and the one where you feel more comfortable in the free sample test.

Can I use the Duolingo English Test for a UK visa?

For visa purposes the UK government only accepts approved SELT tests, including IELTS for UKVI; the Duolingo English Test and TOEFL are not on that list. A university may accept other tests for its own admissions — check the specific programme and the current list on gov.uk.

What changed in TOEFL scores in 2026?

Since January 2026, TOEFL iBT scores are on a 1–6 scale, in half steps, mapped directly onto CEFR levels (4–4.5 = B2, 5–5.5 = C1). The old 0–120 score is still printed on the score report as a comparable score until January 2028.

How long are IELTS and TOEFL results valid?

According to ETS, a TOEFL score is valid for two years; IELTS recommends treating a result as valid for two years, although some organisations accept older ones. Check the validity of the Duolingo English Test on its official website.
