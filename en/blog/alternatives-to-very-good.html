Very good for everything? 20 words and phrases for more natural English ✨

26 October 2026 · Nodar

Your host serves you a delicious dinner — "Very good!" A friend tells you about their new job — "Very good!" A colleague finishes a difficult project — "Very good!" again. There's no grammar mistake anywhere, but using the same phrase in every situation slowly starts to sound boring, and sometimes even a little cold.

Below you'll find synonyms for very good — in other words, different ways to say “good” in English. There are 20 words and phrases in total, numbered and grouped by topic: food, people, work, impressions and short reactions in conversation. At the end there's a summary table and a short quiz.

Why we say very good about everything

In many languages, the word for “very good” fits almost anywhere: food, a film, a person, a finished piece of work. So when you speak English, your brain grabs a ready-made formula and translates it word for word. In English, though, almost every situation has its own usual word: food is delicious, a person is kind, a piece of work is impressive.

An important clarification: very good is not a mistake. Your English is very good is a perfectly natural compliment. The problem starts when there simply isn't any other way of praising something in your vocabulary: your speech becomes monotonous and can no longer carry emotion.

Food: delicious, tasty, lovely

1. delicious — very tasty. It's a strong word: the “very” is already built into it.
2. tasty — good to eat. It's more conversational and less emotional than delicious.
3. lovely — wonderful, really nice. British people say this word a lot — about food, the weather and people. In America you'll hear it less often.

✅ This soup is delicious!
✅ These pastries are really tasty.
✅ Thank you for a lovely dinner.

Now for the most common trap. Since delicious already means “very tasty”, very sounds less natural with it. To strengthen an adjective like this, use really or absolutely:

⚠️ This cake is very delicious. — you'll hear it sometimes and it isn't a mistake, but it sounds less natural.
✅ This cake is absolutely delicious!
✅ This cake is really delicious.
✅ This cake is very tasty. — very sits perfectly well with tasty.

People: kind, talented, helpful, thoughtful

You can say He's a very good person in English, but people more often say exactly in what way someone is good.

4. kind — warm-hearted, caring towards others.
5. talented — gifted, with a natural ability.
6. helpful — someone who is happy to help you (about advice or information — “useful”).
7. thoughtful — considerate, caring: someone who thought ahead about what would make you happy.

✅ Our new neighbour is really kind.
✅ Your daughter is so talented!
✅ The hotel staff were very helpful.
✅ Flowers? That's so thoughtful of you!
✅ Anna is lovely — you'll like her.

When you pay someone a compliment, one word is especially confusing: sympathetic doesn't mean “likeable” or “nice” at all — it means showing understanding for someone's problems.

Work and results: excellent, impressive, solid, well done

At work, Very good can sometimes sound a bit like a teacher, as if you were grading your colleague. The four options below express more precisely what you liked.

8. excellent — outstanding. It fits confidently in formal settings, including business emails.
9. impressive — when the result genuinely surprised you.
10. solid — reliable, good quality: no serious flaws. It's positive, but restrained.
11. well done — “good job!” You say it to someone who has done something well. (For a steak, well done means cooked through.)

✅ Thank you for your excellent work on this project.
✅ You ran a marathon? That's impressive!
✅ It's a solid report — just check the last table.
✅ You passed your driving test? Well done!

Impressions: great, amazing, fantastic, brilliant, awesome

To express emotion about a film, a trip, a concert or a piece of news:

12. great — the most universal: for a day, an idea, a film, a person.
13. amazing — astonishing, wonderful.
14. fantastic — superb, wonderful.
15. brilliant 🇬🇧 — in British conversation it often just means “great, that's really good”: Brilliant, thanks! For an American, brilliant means something closer to “outstanding, genius” — that's how they describe an idea or someone's mind.
16. awesome 🇺🇸 — American and informal. You hear it in Britain too, especially among young people, but it won't do in a business email.

✅ We had a great time in Batumi.
✅ The view from the top was amazing.
✅ The concert was fantastic!
✅ 🇬🇧 "I've booked the table." — "Brilliant, thanks!"
✅ 🇺🇸 You got the job? That's awesome!
⚠️ Dear Mr Brown, your proposal is awesome. — too conversational for a business email.
✅ Dear Mr Brown, thank you for your excellent proposal.

Notice: amazing, fantastic and brilliant are strong adjectives, just like delicious, so they also go well with really or absolutely.

Reactions in conversation: Sounds great, Nice one, Perfect, Not bad

In real conversation people often reply with just a word or two. This is exactly where our Very good tends to pop up — and exactly where it's best replaced.

17. Sounds great — about a plan or an offer: “OK, it's a deal!”
18. Nice one! — British conversational praise, a casual “well done!”. You say it to someone who has done something clever or made a good joke. It's also used ironically when someone has messed something up: You broke it? Nice one!
19. Perfect — “ideal, exactly what I need”. You often hear it when confirming an arrangement.
20. Not bad — its meaning depends on intonation. British people are restrained when they praise, so Not bad! or Not bad at all! said with enthusiasm often means a real compliment: “That's really good!” Said with a shrug, though, it means “it's OK, nothing special”.

A: Shall we meet at the café at six? — B: Sounds great!
A: I fixed the printer. — B: Nice one!
A: I'll send you the file tonight. — B: Perfect, thanks.
✅ I made this pizza myself. — Wow, not bad at all! — meaning: it turned out really well! (praise)
⚠️ How was the hotel? — Not bad. — in a flat tone: “it was OK”, neither good nor bad.

Short reactions like these keep a conversation going naturally. If you'd like to handle chat about the weather, the weekend or work just as easily, have a look at the article on small talk.

Instead of very: really, absolutely, pretty

Sometimes you don't need to change the adjective — it's enough to replace very. These three words aren't part of the 20 above: on their own they don't praise anything, they only strengthen or soften an adjective.

really goes with any adjective, while absolutely goes with strong or “absolute” adjectives (absolutely delicious, absolutely right, but not absolutely good). I explain why in detail in the article on too and very.

pretty means “fairly, quite”. Pretty good isn't “very good”: you liked it, but you're not thrilled. It's a positive verdict, just a moderate one.

✅ A: How was the film? — B: Pretty good. — (I liked it, but it wasn't amazing)

Test yourself

Choose the most natural option:

1. A: Let's meet at the station at ten. — B: ___ (a) Sounds great! (b) Delicious!
2. The film was ___ good, but I wouldn't watch it again. (a) absolutely (b) pretty
3. Thank you for helping me move house. You've been so ___. (a) helpful (b) talented
4. Mmm, this cake is ___ delicious! (a) very (b) absolutely
5. Formal email: "Dear Ms Adams, thank you for your ___ presentation." (a) awesome (b) excellent

Answers: 1. (a) — that's how you reply to a plan. 2. (b) — “fairly good”, though you don't want to see it again; absolutely doesn't go with good. 3. (a) — the person helped you. 4. (b) — delicious is a strong adjective. 5. (b) — awesome is too conversational for a formal email.

One practical tip: pick three words from the table that you haven't used yet, and this week use each of them at least once in a real conversation or message. Frequently asked questions

Can I say very delicious?

You'll hear it sometimes, but it sounds less natural: delicious already means very tasty. Really delicious or absolutely delicious is better; very works fine with tasty — very tasty.

Is awesome OK in a formal email?

No. Awesome is an American, informal word — great when chatting with a friend, but in a business email excellent or impressive is better.

Is pretty good good or bad?

It's positive, but moderate: pretty good means fairly good, not very good — you liked it, but you're not thrilled.

Is Not bad a compliment?

It depends on the intonation. Said with enthusiasm, Not bad! or Not bad at all! often means real praise; in a flat tone it means it's OK, nothing special.
