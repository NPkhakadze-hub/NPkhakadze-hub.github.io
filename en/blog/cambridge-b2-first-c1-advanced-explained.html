Cambridge B2 First and C1 Advanced — what they are and who needs them 🎓

26 October 2026 · Nodar

“Should I take FCE or go straight for IELTS? And what's CAE — isn't it the same thing?” — I often get these questions from people who need a certificate for study, for work or simply to prove their level. Cambridge exams aren't as well known as IELTS, but one feature makes them stand out sharply: the certificate doesn't expire.

Below I'll explain simply what B2 First and C1 Advanced are, how the exam is structured, how it's scored and who needs which one. One caveat up front: exam rules change from time to time, so check everything on the official Cambridge website before you register.

What B2 First and C1 Advanced are — and why people still search for FCE and CAE

Both exams belong to the Cambridge English Qualifications family. Each exam in this family is aimed at one specific level. In September 2017 they were renamed: Cambridge English: First (FCE) became B2 First, and Cambridge English: Advanced (CAE) became C1 Advanced. So FCE and B2 First are the same exam — it's just that the new name shows the CEFR level directly. The old abbreviations still appear a lot in textbooks and job ads.

This is where the main difference from IELTS shows: IELTS is one test for all levels and scores you on a 0–9 scale (bands), while a Cambridge exam tests one level. That's why you need to know your own level first. If you're not sure, the guide to levels and the level test will help.

❌ "I have passed B2 First last year with grade B."
✅ "I passed B2 First last year with a grade B." — With a specific time reference (last year), the Present Perfect isn't used — we say I passed, not I have passed. And with grade B it's more natural to add a (a grade B, or simply a B). Cambridge writes the official name of the exam without an article (B2 First); the usually appears only together with the word: the B2 First exam.

Four papers: what awaits you on exam day

Both exams have four papers, and the format is the same in the computer-based and paper-based versions. The timings are:

  • Reading and Use of English — B2 First: 1 h 15 min (7 parts) · C1 Advanced: 1 h 30 min (8 parts). Along with reading, this paper also tests grammar and vocabulary.
  • Writing — B2 First: 1 h 20 min · C1 Advanced: 1 h 30 min. There are two tasks. The first is a compulsory essay; for the second you choose from several options: in B2 First — an article, a letter/email, a review or a report; in C1 Advanced — a letter, a proposal, a report or a review. Each text is 140–190 words in B2 First and 220–260 in C1 Advanced.
  • Listening — about 40 minutes in both exams, 4 parts; you hear each recording twice.
  • Speaking — B2 First: 14 minutes · C1 Advanced: 15 minutes. This time isn't per candidate, but per pair of candidates.

Speaking deserves special attention, because this is where Cambridge differs most from IELTS: you speak together with another candidate (sometimes there are three of you), and there are two examiners. One asks you questions (and also assesses you), while the other only listens and assesses you in detail. In one of the parts, you and your partner have to discuss together and reach a joint decision — so how you listen to your partner and respond to their ideas is assessed as well.

✅ "That's a good point, but don't you think it would be too expensive?"
✅ "What about the second option? Shall we go for that one?"

Scoring: the Cambridge English Scale and what happens if you don't reach the score you need

Your result is reported on the Cambridge English Scale — each paper gets its own score, and the overall score goes on the certificate. The scores correspond to levels like this:

📊 B2 First: 180–190 = Grade A (C1 level) · 173–179 = Grade B (B2) · 160–172 = Grade C (B2) · 140–159 = B1 level certificate
C1 Advanced: 200–210 = Grade A (C2 level) · 193–199 = Grade B (C1) · 180–192 = Grade C (C1) · 160–179 = B2 level certificate

This is the nicest thing about Cambridge: if you just miss the target level, you still get a certificate, only one level lower — B1 for B2 First, B2 for C1 Advanced. A score below that only appears on the Statement of Results — no certificate is issued. On the other hand, a very high score confirms a level one step above the target. However, a C2 level achieved in C1 Advanced doesn't mean a C2 Proficiency certificate: C2 Proficiency is a separate exam.

According to the official website, results for the computer-based exam are released within 5–10 working days, and for the paper-based version usually within 4–6 weeks.

Certificate validity: it doesn't expire, but…

Cambridge states officially that its certificate doesn't expire. IELTS and TOEFL results, on the other hand, are usually accepted for two years. But “doesn't expire” and “accepted everywhere” are two different things: a university, employer or other organisation decides for itself how old a result it will accept, and some ask for a fairly recent one. So if your certificate is several years old, check with the specific institution before you apply.

B2 First, C1 Advanced or IELTS — who needs which

Study. C1 Advanced is accepted by a huge number of universities, B2 First by some programmes, mainly those where B2 is enough. You'll only find the exact answer on the page of the programme you want; I've written separately about planning your studies: English for studying abroad.

Work. For many employers a Cambridge certificate is good proof of your level, and the fact that it doesn't expire is especially handy here: you can still list it on your CV years later.

Visas. Be careful here. For those UK visas that require a language test, the government only accepts approved SELT tests (e.g. IELTS for UKVI) — B2 First and C1 Advanced aren't on that list. The exception: on a Student visa, if you're going to study at bachelor's level or higher, the university can assess your English itself (at least B2 must be confirmed) — and then C1 Advanced is often accepted too. Check this with the specific university. Other countries have their own lists too. So always check the requirements for a particular visa on the official government website. A country-by-country comparison of three tests — IELTS, TOEFL and Duolingo — is in a separate article.

In short: if the specific institution asks for IELTS, take IELTS. If you have a choice and want permanent proof of your level, Cambridge is a good option.

How to prepare: three typical Use of English tasks

In the Reading and Use of English paper, the Use of English tasks have no equivalent in IELTS, so these are the tasks that need the most practice. Three of them catch candidates out especially often:

Open cloze — you write one word in each gap in a text, with no options to choose from. As a rule, it's a preposition, an article, a conjunction, an auxiliary verb, a pronoun or part of a fixed expression.

"I've lived in Tbilisi ___ 2019." → ✅ since

Word formation — you form the correct word from the one given: a noun, an adjective, an adverb, sometimes one with a negative meaning.

"The ___ of the new bridge took three years." (CONSTRUCT) → ✅ construction

Key word transformation — you rewrite a sentence in other words without changing its meaning; you must use the word given and you mustn't change its form. The number of words in the answer is limited (in C1 Advanced — from 3 to 6 words, including the given word), and this task tests grammar and vocabulary at the same time.

"We couldn't go out because of the storm." (PREVENTED)
"The storm ___ out." → ✅ "The storm prevented us from going out."

Knowing the format really does improve your result, but it can't fill gaps in vocabulary and grammar — so the level comes first, technique second. I've explained how targeted online preparation works in this article.

Where to take the exam

Wherever you live, Cambridge exams are only held at authorised centres. I won't list centres, dates or prices here — all of that changes. The most reliable way is the official search: on the Cambridge website, open the Find an exam centre page, choose your country and city, and see which centre runs which exam. Then contact the centre directly and find out the dates, the format (computer-based or paper-based) and the registration deadline.

Test yourself

Key word transformation: complete the second sentence so that the meaning stays the same. Use the word given in capitals, without changing its form.

1. "I haven't seen Nino for three years." (LAST)
"The ______ was three years ago."
2. "Giorgi started learning English when he was ten, and he is still learning it." (BEEN)
"Giorgi ______ English since he was ten."
3. "I'm sure she didn't forget about the meeting." (CAN'T)
"She ______ about the meeting."
4. "Kate is sorry that she didn't accept the job offer." (WISHES)
"Kate ______ the job offer."

Answers: 1. last time I saw Nino (or last time that I saw Nino). 2. has been learning (or 's been learning; has been studying is also acceptable). 3. can't have forgotten (or can't possibly have forgotten). 4. wishes she had accepted (or wishes that she had accepted, wishes she'd accepted; had taken is also acceptable; wishes she hadn't turned down / hadn't refused is correct too).

One practical tip: before you register, do an official Cambridge sample test from start to finish, keeping to the time limits — that way you'll find out which one suits you: B2 First or C1 Advanced. Frequently asked questions

Are FCE and B2 First the same thing?

Yes. In 2017 Cambridge renamed its exams: First (FCE) is now B2 First, and Advanced (CAE) is now C1 Advanced. The exam is the same; the new name simply shows the CEFR level directly.

Does a Cambridge certificate expire?

According to Cambridge, the certificate doesn't expire. However, a university or employer decides for itself how old a result it will accept, and some ask for a fairly recent one — check with the specific institution before you apply.

What happens if I don't reach the required score in B2 First or C1 Advanced?

If your score is just below the target, you still get a certificate, only one level lower: with 140–159 in B2 First a B1 certificate, with 160–179 in C1 Advanced a B2 certificate. A lower score only appears on the Statement of Results — no certificate is issued.

Which is better — C1 Advanced or IELTS?

What matters is what the receiving institution asks for. If a programme or authority asks for IELTS, take IELTS; for those UK visas that require a language test, an approved SELT test is required (e.g. IELTS for UKVI), and C1 Advanced isn't on that list. The exception is a Student visa for study at bachelor's level or higher: there the university can assess your English itself and may accept C1 Advanced — check this with the university. If you have a choice and want permanent proof of your level, C1 Advanced is a good option.
